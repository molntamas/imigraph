1. The rules

Every walker is a ball {w,s,v}: a walker node on site s with velocity v, a node of the velocity lattice (a ball at rest sits on the origin o). Each rule is a rewrite of ball hyperedges in the notation {w,s,v}_k, {s,t}_k → {w,t,v}: the pattern on the left, what replaces it on the right; the annotations say which edges of the room and of the velocity lattice are meant. The pattern drawings show the variables as nodes (sites grey, walkers red, velocities blue) and the edges of each side with their annotations. Direction weights (+x, −x, +y, −y) and the self-loop policy of a rule apply to its drawn edges. Velocities of this run: $velocity, ball radius: $radius, overlapping matches: $overlaps.

4. Measured against the prediction

$measured_table

Few walkers and walls make the measured values noisy; the prediction holds far from the walls, for many walkers and many steps. A rule with a drift or more hops per step is meant to differ from the uniform random hop. Moving balls have no diffusion prediction; their momentum and energy are measured instead.

5. Reproduce

The session as the GUI exports it. Save it as session.json and run python -m imigraph playground-report --request-file session.json --label <name>, or import it in the GUI.
