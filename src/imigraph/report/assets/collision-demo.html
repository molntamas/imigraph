Two hyperedge walkers on a 4×4 world with reflecting walls, the HPP rules (collisions hpp, Reflect, Stream). Walker 0 starts at (0,1) heading +x, walker 1 at (2,1) heading −x: two sites apart, straight at each other. Play the animation and read the caption of every step; the same captions appear under the step-by-step animation of every hyperedge run in the playground.

  1. Step 1, Stream. Each walker moves one edge along its heading. Both arrive at (1,1), one heading +x, the other −x: a head-on pair, the only configuration Collide acts on. They may share the site because the exclusion principle is about channels (site + heading), not sites.
  2. Step 2, Collide, then Stream. Collide runs first in every step. It finds the head-on pair at (1,1), checks that the perpendicular channels +y and −y are empty and are real edges (not wall self-loops), and rewrites {w0,s,+x}, {w1,s,−x} → {w0,s,+y}, {w1,s,−y} (or the other way round, drawn at random). Then Stream moves them apart to (1,2) and (1,0). The site is ringed in the animation. Momentum before: (+1) + (−1) = 0 along x; after: (+1) + (−1) = 0 along y. Every walker keeps its speed, one lattice spacing per step, so the kinetic energy is unchanged. Nothing else can happen to a head-on pair on a square lattice: the only two-walker states with zero momentum are the x pair and the y pair.
  3. Step 3, Reflect, then Stream. The walker heading −y at (1,0) faces the wall: its −y edge is a self-loop. Reflect turns it round to +y, the wall takes the momentum (as a specular wall does for a hard disk), and Stream moves it back to (1,1). The other walker streams on to (1,3).
  4. Steps 4 and 5. The walker at (1,3) reflects at the top wall and comes back heading −y, meets the other one heading +y at (1,2): a head-on pair again, and Collide sends them out along ±x. The caption shows the total momentum at every step: it changes only in the steps with a wall reflection, never in a collision.

What the rule does not do, deliberately. Two walkers meeting at right angles (+x and +y on one site) pass through each other: on a square lattice there is no other two-walker state with the same momentum, so a momentum-conserving rule cannot do anything with them. Two head-on walkers on neighbouring sites swap sites in one Stream and never share a site, so they do not collide either: the HPP lattice splits into two sub-lattices that never interact (a known artefact of HPP, fixed by the hexagonal FHP lattice, which is the next step on the roadmap). A chain rule on hyperedge walkers (a random hop) is not a collision: it injects momentum at random, like a heat bath; for a conserving gas use Stream and Reflect only. Try it: load this world into the playground, change a heading or a starting site, and watch which steps still collide.
