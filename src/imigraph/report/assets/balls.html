How the ball gas works, and how it is held against the reference

Each ball is one walker node (radius 0) or the lattice points within a radius, attached to sites of the room, with an integer velocity (vx, vy) in cells per step. Between contacts a ball moves as the single bouncing ball does: a straight line hop by hop, and a specular, elastic bounce when a hop would enter a wall self-loop. All balls hop in rounds, one hop each, so a fast ball takes more rounds than a slow one.

After every round the balls are checked for contact: two balls sharing a site, or two balls that crossed each other along an edge. What happens then is the collision rule, chosen above:

rulewhat happens at a contactconserves
exchangethe elastic collision of equal masses. The line of centres before the hop is a lattice direction d, an axis or a diagonal. The velocity component along d is exchanged: va' = va − δ d, vb' = vb + δ d with δ = (va − vb)·d / d·d. Along an axis δ is an integer and the result is exact. Along a diagonal d·d = 2, and when (va − vb)·d is odd the exact result is not a lattice velocity: the balls then swap their whole velocity vectors instead (the head-on outcome). These fallback collisions are counted and shown; they conserve momentum and energy exactly but scatter at the wrong angle. Separating pairs do not collide. momentum and energy, exactly
swapthe balls always exchange their whole velocity vectors, whatever the geometry: every hit is treated as head-on.momentum and energy, exactly
nonethe balls pass through each other: an ideal gas of points. Sites may be shared. momentum and energy (nothing happens)

Under exchange and swap the hop that produced the contact is undone for both balls, so no two balls ever share a site, and the balls continue the step with their new velocities. Later, particles made of several nodes and edges may carry their own collision rules; the rule is a configuration of the gas for that reason.

The reference: a hard-disk gas

The traditional simulation is the hard-disk gas of the gas report: disks in continuous space moving in straight lines, elastic collisions that exchange the velocity components along the line of centres, specular walls, integrated with a small time step. It starts exactly where the balls start, with the balls' velocities, with a disk covering each ball's cells, and it is recorded at the lattice steps, so the 2D projection of the graph system (the ball centres) and the reference are compared frame by frame: the same energy and momentum bookkeeping, the same occupancy entropy, the same speed distribution, and the distribution checks of the comparison framework (mean squared displacement, marginals, occupancy). Where the two differ, the lattice is the reason: the balls' velocities are quantised to integers, their collisions happen at lattice sites rather than at the exact contact time, and odd diagonal hits take the swap fallback. With few balls the entropy and Maxwell checks are informative only (they are statistical statements about many particles); with a single ball the room is the only thing to collide with, which is the bouncing ball of before.
