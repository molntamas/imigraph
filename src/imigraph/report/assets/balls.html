How the ball gas works, and how it is held against the reference

Each ball is the one kind of walker of this project, {w,s,v}: a walker node (radius 0) or the lattice points within a radius, attached to a site of the room and to a node of the velocity lattice, an integer velocity (vx, vy) in cells per step. A step moves every ball hop by hop in rounds, so a fast ball takes more rounds than a slow one, and the rules of the chosen collision rule set are tried in order in every round; the same rules can be edited and run in the playground above. Every rule is a graph rewrite of ball hyperedges (the notation is explained with the playground); none of the physics is a formula in the code:

Exchange moves the two velocity nodes to the other corners of the rectangle they span along the lattice direction d of the line of centres: the components along d are exchanged, the perpendicular ones kept. A contact is found before the hop: two balls whose next hops would meet (land on one site, hit a ball that cannot move, or cross one edge) and that approach each other collide where they stand, and leave with their new velocities in the same round; a meeting that no rule resolves makes the balls wait, so no two balls ever share a site. A ball whose velocity changed continues the step with the remaining hops of the new velocity, never more hops than it had. Later, particles made of several nodes and edges may carry their own collision rules; the rule set is a configuration of the gas for that reason.

Watch a collision

Choose the placement two balls head-on: ball 0 starts at the left wall with the largest speed, ball 1 at the right wall with speed 1, on the same row. Play step by step and read the caption: free flight, then the contact, where Exchange swaps the x components exactly (ball 0 leaves with speed 1, ball 1 with the larger speed, the total momentum unchanged), then the wall bounces by Reflect, where a component flips and the total momentum changes. The reference on the right does the same in continuous space; the two stay in step until a lattice collision happens at a site rather than at the exact contact time. Two balls at right angles shows a diagonal hit: with speeds 1 and 1 Exchange is exact, change one speed to 2 and Swap takes the pair, which the caption and the collision count say.

The reference: a hard-disk gas

The traditional simulation is the hard-disk gas of the gas report: disks in continuous space moving in straight lines, elastic collisions resolved at the exact time of impact (the disks are moved back to contact, the velocity components along the line of centres are exchanged, and they move on), specular walls, integrated with a small time step, so positions are continuous and no disk is ever pushed. It starts exactly where the balls start, with the balls' velocities, with a disk covering each ball's cells, and it is recorded at the lattice steps, so the 2D projection of the graph system (the ball centres) and the reference are compared frame by frame: the same energy and momentum bookkeeping, the same occupancy entropy, the same speed distribution, and the distribution checks of the comparison framework (mean squared displacement, marginals, occupancy). Where the two differ, the lattice is the reason: the balls' velocities are quantised to integers, their collisions happen at lattice sites rather than at the exact contact time, and odd diagonal hits take Swap. With few balls the entropy and Maxwell checks are informative only (they are statistical statements about many particles); with a single ball the room is the only thing to collide with: a ball bouncing in a room.
