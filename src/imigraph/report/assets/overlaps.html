Overlapping matches

A chain rule matches once per walker: the walker edge {w,s0} plus the chain of site edges {s0,s1}, … that the walker drew with the direction weights. Two matches overlap when they share a site edge: two walkers on one site that drew the same edge, or two chains that cross the same edge (a wall self-loop {s,s} is an edge too). In a Wolfram-style rewriting system a match consumes its whole left-hand side, so overlapping matches cannot all be applied in one generation, and the system needs a rule for which ones are. Here that rule is the overlap strategy, part of the rule configuration:

strategywhat happens in a stepeffect
parallelevery match is applied. The rule rewrites only the walker edge, the site edges are read and left in place, so sharing them is harmless.all walkers move at once; walkers never wait. The prediction of drift and diffusion assumes this.
orderedmatches are applied one after the other in walker order (walker 0 first). A match whose chain shares a site edge with a match already applied in this step is skipped: its walker waits and is not rewritten. low walker indices get priority on contested edges; where walkers crowd, fewer hops per step than predicted.
randomlike ordered, in an order drawn afresh every step.fair on average; the same throttling where walkers crowd.
oldestlike ordered, but the walker that has waited longest goes first (ties by index): the event-age ordering of the Wolfram models, in which the least recently updated part of the graph is rewritten first. no walker starves: two walkers fighting over one edge alternate.

Waiting hops are counted in the measured values, and a waiting walker's match is drawn as a stay in the rule animation. The built-in rules match every walker on its own hyperedge and never overlap; the pairs of Collide are disjoint by the exclusion principle. The overlap strategy decides which matches are applied (an input conflict); the exclusion update of hyperedge walkers decides which results fit (an output conflict); the two are independent. The comparison run of section 2 always uses the parallel strategy.
