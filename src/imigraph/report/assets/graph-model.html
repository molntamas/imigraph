The graph model: questions and answers

What type of graph is this? Two kinds of nodes. The space is a static square lattice: site nodes joined by edges that carry a direction label (+x, −x, +y, −y; the lattice is used as the Cayley graph of ℤ²), with a self-loop at every boundary site for each neighbour it lacks (the walls) or, on a periodic lattice, wrap-around edges instead. Each particle is a separate walker node. Walker nodes connect only to site nodes, so the combined graph is bipartite between the two types; the types are explicit (WalkerGraph.to_networkx() marks every node kind="space" or kind="walker"). The random-hop walker is attached by one plain edge {w,s}. The lattice-gas walker is attached by an ordered hyperedge {w,s,s′}: its position s and its heading s′, which is its velocity.

How are energy, momentum and entropy computed in the graph simulation? Random hop: a walker has no velocity of its own, so the kinetic energy and the momentum of a step are read off the displacement Δr of that step: m |Δr|² / (2τ²) and m Δr / τ, averaged per recording interval. They are stationary ensemble values (the temperature 2mD/τ and a momentum that fluctuates around zero), not conserved quantities: the walker trades them with the implicit bath, like a Brownian particle. Lattice gas: every walker moves one spacing per step, so its speed is c = a/τ and its energy mc²/2 never changes; the total momentum is mc ∑ ek over the direction labels of all walkers. Entropy is the same in both: the Shannon entropy of the occupancy histogram of the projected positions on a coarse grid, which rises from a concentrated start towards ln(bins) for a uniform occupancy.

What happens when graph particles collide? Random hop: nothing. The rule's pattern contains a single walker, so two walkers on one site never see each other and pass straight through; this is right for independent Brownian particles. Lattice gas: the Collide rule {w1,s,a}, {w2,s,b}, {s,c}, {s,d} → {w1,s,c}, {w2,s,d}, {s,c}, {s,d} fires when two walkers on one site head in opposite directions and the perpendicular channels are free: the pair leaves at right angles. Momentum (a + b = 0 = c + d), energy and the exclusion principle (one walker per channel) are conserved. Walkers that cross, move in parallel or meet at right angles do not interact, exactly as in the HPP lattice gas; a walker heading into a wall self-loop is reflected and hands its momentum to the wall.
