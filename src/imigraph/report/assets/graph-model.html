The graph model: questions and answers

What type of graph is this? Three kinds of nodes. The room is a static square lattice: site nodes joined by edges that carry a direction label (+x, −x, +y, −y; the lattice is used as the Cayley graph of ℤ²), with a self-loop at every boundary site for each neighbour it lacks (the walls) or, on a periodic lattice, wrap-around edges instead. The velocity lattice is a second static square lattice, the integer vectors (vx, vy) with the same direction labels on its edges and the origin o for rest. Each particle is a separate walker node, attached by one ordered hyperedge {w,s,v} to the site s it sits on and the velocity node v it moves with. Walker nodes connect only to those two lattices, so the combined graph is tripartite; the types are explicit (WalkerGraph.to_networkx() marks every node kind="space" or kind="walker" and keeps the velocity as an attribute). There is one kind of walker, the ball; the random walker is a ball on the origin of the velocity lattice, the lattice-gas walker a ball on a unit vector.

How are energy, momentum and entropy computed in the graph simulation? Random hop: a ball at rest has no velocity of its own, so the kinetic energy and the momentum of a step are read off the displacement Δr of that step: m |Δr|² / (2τ²) and m Δr / τ, averaged per recording interval. They are stationary ensemble values (the temperature 2mD/τ and a momentum that fluctuates around zero), not conserved quantities: the walker trades them with the implicit bath, like a Brownian particle. Moving balls: the velocity v is in cells per step, so the speed is |v| a/τ, the kinetic energy k m |v|² a² / (2τ²) for a body of k nodes and the momentum k m v a/τ, both integers times a unit, both summed over the balls. Entropy is the same in every case: the Shannon entropy of the occupancy histogram of the projected positions on a coarse grid, which rises from a concentrated start towards ln(bins²) for a uniform occupancy.

What happens when graph particles collide? Only what a rule says. The chain rules have a single ball in their pattern, so two walkers on one site never see each other and pass straight through; this is right for independent Brownian particles. A collision rule has two balls whose next hops meet (they would land on one site, cross one edge, or already share a site) and rewrites their velocity nodes by the geometry of the velocity lattice: Exchange moves the pair to the other two corners of the rectangle they span along the line of centres (the elastic collision of equal masses, exact), Swap exchanges the two nodes (the head-on outcome), Turn rotates a head-on pair by a quarter turn (the HPP collision). Each conserves momentum and energy by construction; a ball about to hop into a wall self-loop is reflected (its velocity mirrored) and hands its momentum to the wall.
