Hard-disk gas. $gas_particles disks move in straight lines. When two disks overlap while approaching each other, the velocity components along the line of centres are exchanged (an elastic collision of equal masses), which conserves momentum and kinetic energy exactly; the overlap is then removed. Walls are specular. Collision times are quantised to the time step, which never changes the energy.

Lattice gas on the graph. $lattice_particles balls live on a $lattice_size×$lattice_size lattice graph. A ball is the hyperedge {w,s,v}: a walker node on site s with its velocity v, a node of the velocity lattice; here every v is a unit vector, so every ball has the speed c = a/τ. Four rules are applied to all balls at once each step: Reflect (a ball about to hop into a wall self-loop mirrors that velocity component), Turn (two balls whose hops meet head-on turn by a quarter turn), Swap (any other pair whose hops meet swaps velocities, a deflection where HPP's point particles would pass through each other) and Stream (every ball moves one edge along its velocity). This is the HPP lattice gas written as graph rewriting, the collision taken at the contact rather than on a shared site.

The experiment. Both gases start in the left half of the box with speed 1 and expand into the empty half; both are recorded every 0.1 time units. Energy must stay constant, the momentum must stay bounded (walls take momentum) or constant (periodic walls), the occupancy entropy must rise to the uniform value, and particles must have collided ($gas_collisions disk collisions, $lattice_collisions lattice collisions in this run). The hard-disk gas also relaxes to the Maxwell–Boltzmann speed law; the lattice gas cannot, because every ball keeps the speed c, and the page says so instead of hiding it.

2. The lattice-gas rules, step by step

Reflect {w,s,v}_k, {s,s}_k, {v,v′}|k → {w,s,v′}: a ball whose next hop k runs into a wall self-loop {s,s}_k gets the mirror image v′ of its velocity across the axis of k; the wall takes the momentum. On a periodic lattice the rule never matches and the total momentum is exact.

Turn {w1,s,v1}_k1, {w2,t,v2}_k2, {s,x}_k1, {t,x}_k2, {v1,v2}|o, {v1,v1′}^, {v1′,v2′}|o → {w1,s,v1′}, {w2,t,v2′}: two balls whose next hops meet at x with opposite velocities (v2 = −v1) turn by a quarter turn (v1′ is v1 turned, either sense at random, and v2′ = −v1′). Momentum 0 before and after, energy unchanged: the HPP collision.

Swap {w1,s,v1}_k1, {w2,t,v2}_k2, {s,x}_k1, {t,x}_k2 → {w1,s,v2}, {w2,t,v1}: any other pair whose next hops meet swaps velocities, each ball continuing the other's motion from its own site: a deflection, where the point particles of HPP would pass through each other and share the site.

Stream {w,s,v}_k, {s,t}_k → {w,t,v}: every ball moves one edge along the direction k of its velocity. The four rules are applied in this order to all balls at once; together they are one time step τ.

Six balls on a 7×7 lattice, every ball in its own colour with its number. Arrows: velocities. Orange rings: balls whose velocity a rule changed in the step that leads to the shown frame. The first collision happens at the centre; the ball at the right edge reflects.

The distribution checks of the comparison framework, run on the two projected trajectories at the same recording times.
