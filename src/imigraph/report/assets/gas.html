Hard-disk gas. $gas_particles disks move in straight lines. When two disks overlap while approaching each other, the velocity components along the line of centres are exchanged (an elastic collision of equal masses), which conserves momentum and kinetic energy exactly; the overlap is then removed. Walls are specular. Collision times are quantised to the time step, which never changes the energy.

Lattice gas on the graph. $lattice_particles walkers live on a $lattice_size×$lattice_size lattice graph. A walker is the hyperedge {w,s,s′}: it sits on site s and heads for the neighbour s′ along an edge with a direction label, so it has a velocity of fixed speed c = a/τ. Three rules are applied to all walkers at once each step: Collide (a head-on pair on one site leaves at right angles), Reflect (a walker heading into a wall self-loop turns round) and Stream (every walker moves one edge). At most one walker per channel (site, direction). This is the HPP lattice gas written as graph rewriting.

The experiment. Both gases start in the left half of the box with speed 1 and expand into the empty half; both are recorded every 0.1 time units. Energy must stay constant, the momentum must stay bounded (walls take momentum) or constant (periodic walls), the occupancy entropy must rise to the uniform value, and particles must have collided ($gas_collisions disk collisions, $lattice_collisions lattice collisions in this run). The hard-disk gas also relaxes to the Maxwell–Boltzmann speed law; the lattice gas cannot, because every walker keeps the speed c, and the page says so instead of hiding it.

2. The lattice-gas rules, step by step

Collide {w1,s,a}, {w2,s,b}, {s,c}, {s,d} → {w1,s,c}, {w2,s,d}, {s,c}, {s,d}: two walkers on site s heading in opposite directions a, b, whose perpendicular channels c, d are empty real edges, leave along c and d (which walker takes c is random). Momentum a + b = 0 = c + d and energy are unchanged; the number of walkers per channel stays at most one.

Reflect {w,s,s} → {w,s,s′}: a walker heading into a self-loop (a wall) turns round; the wall takes the momentum. On a periodic lattice the rule never matches and the total momentum is exact.

Stream {w,s,s′}k, {s′,s″}k → {w,s′,s″}k: every walker moves one edge and keeps its direction label k. The three rules are applied in this order to all walkers simultaneously; together they are one time step τ.

Six walkers on a 7×7 lattice. Arrows: walkers and their headings. Orange rings: sites where Collide fired in the step that leads to the shown frame. The first collision happens at the centre in step 2; the walker at the right edge reflects.
