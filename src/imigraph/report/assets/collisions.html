The ball, the two lattices and the rule language

There is one kind of walker, the ball {w,s,v}: a walker node w, the site s of the room it sits on, and its velocity v, which is a node of a second lattice, the velocity lattice of integer vectors (vx, vy) in cells per step. Both lattices carry the same direction labels +x, −x, +y, −y on their edges; the room has wall self-loops {s,s} where a neighbour is missing. A ball at rest sits on the origin o of the velocity lattice: that is the random walker of the Brownian imitation. A ball with a unit velocity is the walker of the lattice gas. A ball may have a body, the lattice points within a radius of its site, which moves rigidly with it.

A step moves every ball by its velocity, one hop per round: a ball with velocity (2, 1) takes three hops (two along x, one along y, interleaved so the path hugs the straight line), a ball at rest takes none. The rules whose balls carry a hop label _k (Stream, Reflect, the collision rules) are round rules: they are tried in order in every round on the balls that still have hops left, each seeing what the previous one did. The other rules (chain rules, Kick) run once per step after the rounds. Every rule is a rewrite of ball hyperedges in one notation; the room and the velocity lattice are only read:

A velocity named on the left side but not bound by a ball (v') is solved: the matcher walks the velocity lattice (components up to the max speed) and keeps the nodes that satisfy every relation. No node means the rule does not match and the next rule gets its chance; several mean one is drawn at random. That is what keeps the collision rules pure graph rewrites, with no formula hidden in the code:

Stream{w,s,v}_k, {s,t}_k → {w,t,v}free flight: one hop per round along the velocity; into a wall self-loop it stays. Conserves everything.

A contact is found before the hop, so no two balls ever share a site. In every round, a ball that cannot move (no hops left, blocked by a wall) stays; two balls whose next hops meet (they would land on one site, one would land on a staying ball, or they would cross one edge) are held back, and a ball that would land on a held ball is held too, until nothing changes. Held pairs that approach each other along their line of centres are the contacts the collision rules match; the rules rewrite their velocities, the pair's new hops are looked at afresh, and the balls continue the step with the remaining hops of the new velocity (never more hops than they had). A meeting that no rule resolves, or a pair that has already collided and separates, makes the balls wait: they do not hop in that round. A collision rule is tried again within the round while it finds new contacts, since a ball redirected by one collision may meet a third. Order matters: Reflect before the collision rules makes a ball at the wall turn before its contact is looked for; Exchange before Swap makes Swap the fallback for the hits Exchange cannot resolve exactly. The HPP lattice gas is Reflect, Turn, Swap, Stream on unit velocities; the textbook model collides head-on pairs on a shared site one hop later and lets other pairs pass through each other, here the collision is taken at the contact (which also lets head-on pairs at odd distance meet) and the other pairs swap velocities, a deflection, since balls cannot share a site. Balls placed on one site ignore each other until they have separated.
