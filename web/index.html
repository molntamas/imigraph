imigraph live: Brownian motion in a box, imitated by a graph

The same Python package as the tests, running in your browser through Pyodide (Python 3.14 in WebAssembly). The first load downloads Pyodide, numpy and scipy (about 15 MB) and may take half a minute.

1. Playground: a small world and an editable rule

Change the world, the number of walkers and the rule, then Apply: the rule diagram and the step-by-step animation are recomputed in Python at once, and the measured diffusion, drift and wall rate are compared with what the rule predicts. Rules are written in the notation of the report: a walker edge {w,s} plus a chain of site edges on the left, the walker re-attached to a site of the chain on the right. The direction weights apply to every chain edge (+x, −x, +y, −y); self-loops decides whether a walker at a wall may stay.

2. Comparison run: does the rule imitate Brownian motion?

The reference always runs with D = a² / (4τ), the value of the uniform random hop. A rule with a drift or a different number of hops is supposed to fail the checks; that is the experiment. Smaller runs finish faster in the browser.

2b. The rule of this run, explained

The space is a lattice graph; boundary nodes keep self-loops for the neighbours they lack (the walls). Each particle is a walker node attached by one edge. The rule picks, for every walker at once, labelled edges from its node with the rule's direction weights and moves the walker edge along the chain; a self-loop means the walker stays.

One walker on a 5×5 space; each application in four phases (state, match, choose, rewrite).

2e. Energy, momentum, entropy, walls, spreading

Energy and momentum are measured from every simulation step and averaged per recording interval; the ensemble values stay the same (temperature 2D/dt, momentum zero within its expected fluctuation) in both systems. The occupancy entropy rises towards ln(bins), on average but not monotonically.
